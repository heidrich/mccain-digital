Background, round two

The first four were all the same idea at four volumes — cells lit at random, drifting. None of them landed, so this round is two different ideas rather than four settings of one.

A dither field is not scattered cells: it is a picture made of enlarged square pixels, thresholded through a 4×4 Bayer matrix, with brightness driven by fractal noise so it forms broad cloud masses. It has structure. It reads as material — as if the band itself were made of the same pixels the headlines are.

Guides are the opposite answer: no motion at all. The page simply admits where its container is, with two hairlines and four corner marks. On a site written in mono labels and (01)/(02) markers that reads as structure, and it costs one paint, forever.

Both borrow from MengTo/Skills (MIT). The dither idea is theirs; the way it is drawn here is not — see the note under row C.

A — off

The control.

What the site does today. The rest have to earn the difference.

B — dither, coarse cell

Cloud, at a distance.

7px cells — deliberately coarser than the 3px the headlines are built from, so the background reads as further away. Bigger pixels, lower resolution, back of the room.

My first choice. The coarser grid keeps it clearly separate from the type instead of competing with it.

C — dither, the headline's own cell

Same grid as the type.

3px cells, exactly the grid the pixel headlines use. Everything on the band is then made of one size of pixel.

Tidier in theory, and finer than the Bayer pattern really wants — at 3px the ordered structure starts to disappear into grain, which is the one thing this effect is supposed not to be.

How it is drawn: the reference implementation calls fillRect once per cell per frame — about 26,000 draw calls a frame at this size, sixty times a second, which is the exact shape of the work that took this page to 20fps once already. Here the field is written into an ImageData, one pixel per cell, and blown up by CSS with image-rendering: pixelated, at 12fps. The enlarged squares come from the scaling, for free.

D — guides, no motion at all

Structure, not atmosphere.

Two hairlines on the container edges and four small squares on the section's real corners. Nothing moves, nothing costs anything after the first paint.

Worth a serious look. It answers "the band looks empty" without adding a single moving thing, and it fits a page that already talks in mono labels and numbered sections. It also combines with any of the others.

E — both

Material and measure.

The coarse dither with the guides on top of it.
