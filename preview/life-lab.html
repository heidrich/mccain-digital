More life — the background, four ways

Owner: „mehr leben". The same 3px cell the headlines are built from, a few per cent of them lit, drifting slowly. It is the page's own material rather than an effect borrowed from somewhere else — and it costs nothing per frame: one tile is drawn once and handed to CSS as a repeating background, and the drift is a transform the compositor can do without repainting.

Scroll through all four. They are full-height bands on purpose: a texture judged in a narrow strip is a texture judged at the wrong scale.

A — off

The control.

What the site does today. Nothing wrong with it — which is the point of having it here: the next three have to earn the difference.

Flat black. Reads as clean on a big screen and as empty on a long scroll.

B — texture, standing still

Material, not motion.

The field is there but never moves. It gives the black a grain, so the band stops looking like an empty rectangle, and it asks nothing of the eye.

The safe one. Costs literally nothing after the first paint — no animation at all, so it is also exactly what a visitor with reduced motion sees in every other candidate.

C — two layers, drifting

Alive, quietly.

Two fields at different densities and speeds. The difference between them is what reads as depth — one layer alone reads as a texture that happens to slide, which is the thing that looks cheap.

The recommendation. A full drift takes about a minute, so nothing ever competes with the type; you notice it the way you notice that a room has air. One tile per layer, one composited transform each, paused off screen.

D — denser, with more of the yellow

Turned up.

The same thing with roughly twice the cells and the accent appearing far more often. Here to mark the far end of the range, so C can be judged against something and not against nothing.

Starts to read as a pattern rather than a surface, and the yellow cells pull the eye away from the yellow that means something. Useful as a boundary.
