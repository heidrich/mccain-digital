MOCKUP — what changed and why

1 · The pixel engine left the type. Dotted stems at display size are hard to read, and the hover scatter was mangling whole words. The engine now runs on the images (they assemble out of a swarm on the way in and re-pixelate under the pointer), on the solid buttons, and it is the logo mark — a 7×7 M on the same grid the engine draws on. It stopped being an effect done to words and became the thing the studio is signed with.

2 · The header is a full-bleed stage and the dither field runs there and nowhere else, as asked. Guide rails and corner marks sit over it, the copy is anchored low-left, the media plate high-right.

3 · The palette lost its brown. The old ground was #0d0c0a — red and green above blue, which reads sepia next to a warm yellow. Now #0b0b0c, neutral, so the logo yellow is the only warm thing on the page. Yellow untouched. The green #7a8c00 is promoted to second signal; it clears AA on the dark ground on its own (5.23:1) and gets a lifted cut for small type.

4 · Measured, not judged. Text over the dither field: coords 7.28:1, fact labels 5.22:1, intro 15.27:1 — all against the brightest ground cell under the line, from the delivered pixels. Headline 15.0:1 white, 10.3:1 yellow. Scroll from the top: 0 long tasks, p90 16.9 ms, 3.1 % of frames over 20 ms.

5 · Still open: the two portraits are honest empty frames, not silhouettes — real photos are the biggest gap before go-live. And the second display face: this runs on Schibsted at weight 800 with the tracking pulled in, which is genuinely strong, but a condensed cut would give the billboard lines another gear for one extra font file.
