The pixel colour wave

Same headline, same grid, same engine — only the palette changes. Under each row is the gradient it generated, painted as a strip: what a wave shows is mostly the colour between its stops, which is exactly where the first attempt went wrong. Hover a headline; the black hole still works and the wave keeps travelling underneath it.

The palette is tiled rather than swept, so it repeats past both edges and no cell is ever outside the gradient. The band version left everything beyond its own width on a clamped end stop — and both end stops are the accent hue, which is why the line kept going back to plain yellow and why the edge of the band looked like a cut through a word.

A — the first attempt, for reference

Two brand colours listed as stops: gold and green. Everything between them is interpolated in sRGB, which drops through a desaturated middle — the strip shows the olive that is neither of the two colours and is most of the span.

F — off

The control. It no longer needs the attribute — "off" is the engine default since the owner took the wave off the h1 — but it stays spelled out, because a control that depends on a default is a control that changes meaning when the default does.

G — the axis: straight across, then leaning

Same palette, same speed, two axes. Straight across reads as a wipe: every pixel in a column carries one colour, so the change arrives as a vertical edge sliding past. Leaning cuts the bands across the letters, which is what an animated gradient border actually looks like. These two rows are also the counter-test for the measurement: the top and the bottom of one column differ by about a third of a hue step when the axis leans, and by nothing at all when it does not — a probe that cannot tell these two apart is not measuring the axis.
